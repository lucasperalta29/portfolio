Cornerstone of Engineering

ShoreClear

Plastic waste accumulates heavily on beaches and in shallow coastal waters, where it threatens marine life, ecosystems, and coastal communities. Existing cleanup methods rely on manual labor or large mechanical systems that are inefficient, costly, and poorly suited for shallow or sensitive environments — creating a need for a practical, low-impact system that can consistently collect debris from shoreline areas before it spreads into deeper water.

ShoreClear was our response to that problem for a first-year engineering course. The main body was modeled in SolidWorks, and the top-mounted collection arm was also designed in SolidWorks before being fabricated through a combination of 3D printing and laser cutting. The tracked mobility base was hand-built from separately purchased components rather than custom-fabricated, keeping the design practical to assemble. Every part of the system — sensing, arm movement, and drive — was wired together and controlled through Arduino boards.
